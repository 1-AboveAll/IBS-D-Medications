This repo compiles the medications (both approved and
off-label) used in the treatment of IBS-D (and some degree SIBO) and their
mechanisms of action. As someone who has suffered from IBS-D for a long time,
I've noticed a disorganization of information online, so I decided to compile
it. Feel free to contribute.

# Pharmacological Treatments for IBS-D

## Antibiotics

- **Rifaximin:** A non-absorbable, broad-spectrum antibiotic. It possesses anti-inflammatory and antibacterial properties and acts locally in the gut. *(FDA-approved for IBS-D)*

- **Metronidazole:** A nitroimidazole antibiotic. Similar to rifaximin, it possesses anti-inflammatory and antibacterial properties, but it is well absorbed and has more systemic side effects.

- **Ornidazole:** Another nitroimidazole antibiotic similar to metronidazole.

---

## Serotonin Antagonists

- **Ondansetron:** Works by blocking peripheral **5-HT3 receptors**, which slows colonic transit time and reduces diarrhea.

- **Alosetron:** Another **5-HT3 receptor antagonist**. *(FDA-approved for IBS-D)*

- **Palonosetron:** Another **5-HT3 receptor antagonist**.

- **Cyproheptadine:** An antihistamine and antiserotonergic drug that reduces overall serotonin activity and may relieve IBS symptoms.

- **L-Lysine:** An essential amino acid that acts as a partial **5-HT4 antagonist**, which may slow intestinal transit.

---

## Bile Acid Sequestrants

- **Cholestyramine:** A bile acid sequestrant that binds bile acids in the gastrointestinal tract to prevent their reabsorption.

- **Colestipol:** Another bile acid sequestrant.

- **Colesevelam:** Another bile acid sequestrant.

---

## Opioids

- **Loperamide:** A **μ-opioid receptor agonist** that works by slowing intestinal movement.

- **Diphenoxylate:** Another opioid agonist used in combination with atropine for the treatment of diarrhea.

- **Eluxadoline:** A **μ- and κ-opioid receptor agonist** that acts locally in the enteric nervous system. *(FDA-approved for IBS-D)*

---

## Neuromodulators

- **Amitriptyline:** A tricyclic antidepressant with mixed effects. It has anticholinergic activity that slows intestinal movement and reduces pain sensitivity.

- **Ketotifen:** A unique mast cell stabilizer with antihistamine, weak anticholinergic, and antiserotonergic activity.

---

## Unclassified Treatments

- **Bismuth:** An antidiarrheal agent with antisecretory and antimicrobial properties.

- **Polymethylsiloxane Polyhydrate (Methylsilicic Acid Hydrogel):** An enterosorbent used for binding and removing toxic substances, infectious agents, and metabolites from the gastrointestinal tract. Available in Europe for IBS-D.

- **Activated Charcoal:** An adsorbent used for poisonings and overdoses. **It is not a standard treatment for IBS-D**, but it may help by absorbing bacteria and slowing intestinal activity.

- **Sucralfate:** Used primarily for duodenal ulcers. It has been studied for prevention of treatment-induced diarrhea and has a very weak absorbent effect similar to bile acid sequestrants.

- **Moro's Carrot Soup:** Created in 1908 as a home remedy for diarrhea. Prolonged cooking of carrots produces acidic oligosaccharides that may help treat diarrhea.

- **Saccharomyces boulardii:** A yeast used for prevention and treatment of antibiotic-associated diarrhea.

- **Racecadotril:** also known as acetorphan, is an antidiarrheal medication which acts as a peripheral enkephalinase inhibitor. It reduces the secretion of water and electrolytes into the intestine.

---

## Antispasmodics

Antispasmodics do not cure IBS, but they can relieve symptoms such as **gas, bloating, and abdominal pain**. They work by relaxing intestinal smooth muscle, primarily through **muscarinic receptor blockade**.
